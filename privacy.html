Roll Call

Privacy Policy

Last updated 16 October 2026

The short version.

  • There are no accounts. We never ask for your email, and we don't have one for you.
  • The photos and videos you submit during a game are uploaded so the other players can see them, and are deleted within an hour of the game ending.
  • Your name and settings live on your phone. Your profile photo is uploaded so players see your face instead of an initial, and stays until you change or remove it.
  • We don't sell your data, and we don't build a profile of you.

Who we are

Roll Call is a party game for phones, made by an independent developer. In this policy "Roll Call", "we" and "us" mean the app and its developer; "you" means the person playing. For anything in here, you can reach us at aryan.projectbase@gmail.com.

What we collect, and why

Roll Call collects as little as it can and keeps almost none of it. Everything below is collected because the game cannot work without it — there is no analytics, advertising profile, or marketing list built from any of it.

Things you type or choose

  • Your display name. Stored on your phone. While you are in a party, it is also stored on our server so the other players' phones can show who is who.
  • Your profile photo, if you add one. This is optional. It is uploaded to our storage and given a public link so the other phones in the party can display it. It stays there until you change it or remove it in Settings.
  • Your app settings — sound, music, haptics. These never leave your phone.

The photos and videos you play with

When a round asks for a photo, you pick one from your camera roll and Roll Call uploads that photo so the rest of the party can see it in the montage and vote on it. We never read, scan, index or upload the rest of your camera roll. The app asks for library access only so you can choose; what you choose is all that is sent.

Uploaded photos and videos are stored with public links, which is what lets the other players' phones display them during a game. Treat anything you submit as visible to everyone in that party.

How the game runs

Scores, votes, round prompts, who the host is, and whether each player is still connected are stored for the duration of a game and deleted with it.

A device identity

Your phone signs in to our database anonymously the first time you open the app. This is a random identifier — not a profile, not linked to anything you typed, and not linked to your Apple or Google account. It exists so the database can tell whether the phone asking to change something is the phone it belongs to. It does not survive deleting the app.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, we receive a crash report through Sentry: the error and where in the code it happened, your device model, your OS version, a random install identifier, and what the game was doing at the time — the round number, the phase, how many people were playing. We use these only to fix bugs.

Crash reports deliberately carry no display names, no party code, and no photos. Screenshots, screen recording and view-hierarchy capture are all switched off, so a crash during a round cannot send us the photo on screen. Reports are kept for a limited period and then deleted.

Advertising

Roll Call shows a full-screen ad at two moments — after you join a party, and on the way out of a finished game — through Google AdMob. Ads can be removed with a one-off purchase.

On iOS, Roll Call asks for your permission before using the Advertising Identifier (IDFA) to personalise those ads. If you say no, you still see ads, they are just not personalised. If you are in the EEA or the UK, you are shown a Google-certified consent form the first time ads load, and you can change your answer later from Settings → Ad Privacy Choices. Google's own handling of ad data is covered by How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Purchases

Prompt packs, the Everything bundle and Remove Ads are one-off purchases, processed through RevenueCat on top of the Apple App Store or Google Play. We never see your card details. RevenueCat receives a random app user identifier and the receipt for what you bought, which is how your purchases come back when you restore them or move to a new phone.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for advertising purposes beyond what is described under Advertising above.

Your choices

  • Don't submit it. Nothing from your camera roll is uploaded unless you pick it for a round.
  • Remove your profile photo at any time in Settings, which deletes the uploaded copy.
  • Clear Local Data in Settings wipes your name and every setting on the phone.
  • Delete the app and the device identity, your name, your photo and your settings go with it. Game data deletes itself on the schedule above.
  • Photo access can be set to limited or off in iOS or Android settings, and Roll Call shows you which state you are in.
  • Ad personalisation can be refused when asked, changed in your device's privacy settings, or — where a consent form applies — revisited under Settings → Ad Privacy Choices.

Your rights

Depending on where you live — in particular in the UK, the EEA, and in US states with privacy laws such as California — you have the right to ask what personal data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to or restrict how we use it, to receive a copy of it, and not to be discriminated against for exercising any of those rights.

In practice, Roll Call holds very little that is tied to you: there is no account to look up, the game data deletes itself within hours, and the device identity is a random value that exists only on your phone and in our database. If you want to make a request anyway, email aryan.projectbase@gmail.com — if you can tell us roughly when you played and the party code, we can find and delete anything still there. If you are in the UK or EEA and are unhappy with our answer, you can complain to your national data protection authority.

Children

Roll Call is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has submitted photos through the app, email us and we will delete what we can find. Note that the game is played on your own camera roll with people in the room, and some prompt packs are written for adults.

Security

Data in transit is encrypted. Our database restricts each phone to the party it has joined and to rows that belong to it. The one deliberate exception is uploaded photos and videos: they are given public links, because that is how the other phones in your party display them. Those links are unguessable and short-lived, but they are not secret — which is why the app tells you, before you submit, that the party will see what you pick.

No system is perfect. If you find a security problem, please email us rather than posting it.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we'll update the date at the top and, where the change is significant, say so in the app. Continuing to play after a change means you accept the updated policy.
